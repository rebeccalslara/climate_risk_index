ECONOMIC IMPACT MODEL REPORT
Climate Risk Index - Santa Catarina Municipalities
Updated: 2026-05-15

================================================================================
1. PURPOSE OF THIS MODELING STEP
================================================================================

The initial goal of this part of the project was to translate variation in the
Climate Risk Index into an economic or fiscal impact that could be communicated
in monetary terms.

The original question was:

    If the Climate Risk Index increases by 0.1, how much does this affect the
    municipality in reais?

The first planned outcome variable was GDP, but this path was later dropped
because the Climate Risk Index already includes economic structure components,
including industrial exposure and an inverted GDP per capita vulnerability
component. This created a theoretical problem: in some municipalities, higher
industrial activity can increase both the Climate Risk Index and GDP, producing
a relationship that is difficult to interpret as climate damage.

The analysis then moved to fiscal impact, using municipal environmental
expenses as the dependent variable.


================================================================================
2. CURRENT DEPENDENT VARIABLE
================================================================================

The current dependent variable is:

    log(real_environmental_expense)

Source:

    data/raw_data/SICONFI/Interface com RSiconfi - Despesas Gestao Ambiental.xlsx

Relevant columns:

    NO_ENTE        municipality name
    AN_EXERCICIO  fiscal year
    VALUE         nominal environmental expense

The file currently covers:

    2015-2023

The nominal expense was converted into real values using IPCA:

    real_environmental_expense = (nominal_environmental_expense / IPCA_index) * 100

Source for deflator:

    data/raw_data/IPEADATA/IPCA.xls

The model uses log(real_environmental_expense), keeping only positive values.
Missing values are not treated as zero. Internal missing gaps are interpolated
only when a municipality has observed values before and after the missing year.


================================================================================
3. MAIN RISK VARIABLE
================================================================================

The main explanatory variable is:

    risk_mean_3yr

This is the mean of:

    risk_norm_t
    risk_norm_lag1
    risk_norm_lag2

The reason for using this 3-year mean is theoretical: climate risk effects and
municipal fiscal responses can be delayed. A current-year-only specification may
miss the way climate stress accumulates or affects budgets with delay.

The interpretation of the risk coefficient is log-linear. For a 0.1 increase in
risk_mean_3yr:

    percent effect = exp(beta * 0.1) - 1


================================================================================
4. CONTROL VARIABLES TESTED
================================================================================

4.1 Transfer revenue control
----------------------------

The main fiscal control retained in the current model is:

    log(real_transfer_revenue)

Source:

    data/raw_data/SICONFI/municipio_receita_transferencia.csv

This control was kept because it is theoretically meaningful. Environmental and
climate-related spending by municipalities often depends on intergovernmental
transfers rather than only on local own-source fiscal capacity.

Transfer revenue is consistently positive and statistically strong in the model.


4.2 Total revenue control
-------------------------

We also tested total municipal revenue:

    log(real_total_revenue)

Source:

    data/raw_data/SICONFI/Interface com RSiconfi - Receitas.xlsx

Filter used:

    tipo_receita == "Receitas Brutas Realizadas"

The nominal total revenue was also deflated using IPCA:

    real_total_revenue = (nominal_total_revenue / IPCA_index) * 100

This control was later removed from the preferred model because:

    - It did not improve the risk coefficient.
    - It was not statistically strong in the full fixed effects model.
    - It overlapped conceptually with transfer revenue.
    - The project theory is more directly related to transferred resources,
      because environmental and climate-related municipal expenses often depend
      on money transferred from higher levels of government.

When total revenue was included, the full FE model produced approximately:

    risk_mean_3yr              coefficient =  0.2634   p-value = 0.8611
    log_real_transfer_revenue  coefficient =  0.8222   p-value = 0.0187
    log_real_total_revenue     coefficient = -0.0130   p-value = 0.6025

Conclusion:

    Total revenue did not strengthen the model and was dropped.


================================================================================
5. FIXED EFFECTS SPECIFICATIONS TESTED
================================================================================

The current script estimates four specifications:

    1. Municipality FE + year FE
    2. Municipality FE only
    3. Year FE only
    4. No fixed effects

The purpose of this comparison is to understand whether the observed
relationship comes from:

    - differences between municipalities, or
    - changes inside the same municipality over time.

Municipality fixed effects absorb all time-invariant municipal characteristics,
including geography, long-term size, structural fiscal capacity, baseline
economic profile, administrative patterns, and stable vulnerability.

Year fixed effects absorb shocks common to all municipalities in a given year,
including macroeconomic conditions, national policy changes, fiscal cycles,
inflation not fully captured by the deflator, and reporting changes.

================================================================================
7. INTERPRETATION OF THE FIXED EFFECTS RESULTS
================================================================================

The results show a clear pattern:

    - Without municipality fixed effects, the risk coefficient is negative and
      statistically significant.

    - With municipality fixed effects, the risk coefficient becomes weak and
      statistically insignificant.

    - Adding year fixed effects alone does not eliminate the negative
      relationship. The year-FE-only model is very similar to the no-FE model.

Therefore, the main source of the relationship is not common annual shocks.
The important change happens when municipality fixed effects are included.

This means the significant negative relationship is mostly cross-sectional:

    municipalities with higher Climate Risk Index scores tend to have lower
    environmental spending, structurally.

It does not mean that an increase in climate risk within the same municipality
causes environmental spending to fall. Instead, it suggests that high-risk
municipalities are structurally different from lower-risk municipalities.

This is coherent with the construction of the Climate Risk Index. High-risk
municipalities often have characteristics such as:

    - lower GDP per capita,
    - stronger agricultural dependence,
    - smaller fiscal capacity,
    - different economic structure,
    - lower ability to finance environmental response.

Because of this, the negative coefficient in the no-FE and year-FE-only models
is not interpreted as a causal climate impact. It is better interpreted as a
possible fiscal response gap:

    municipalities with higher climate risk may not be the municipalities with
    greater environmental spending capacity.


================================================================================
8. WHY THE FULL FE MODEL IS IMPORTANT
================================================================================

The full fixed effects model asks a stricter question:

    Within the same municipality, in years when its climate risk is higher than
    its own average, and after controlling for common year shocks, is
    environmental spending also higher?

The answer from the current data is:

    The coefficient shows a positive relationship, but no statistically robust evidence was found.

This is important because the original goal was to estimate how a change in
climate risk translates into a monetary fiscal impact. The full FE model is the
closest specification to that question, but the current data do not support a
strong conclusion.

Municipality FE + year FE
        coefficient:              0.1563
        p-value:                  0.9176
        effect of +0.1 risk:      +1.58%

================================================================================
9. OTHER DEPENDENT VARIABLES TESTED AND DROPPED
================================================================================

During this stage, other fiscal outcomes were considered or briefly tested:

    - total municipal expenses,
    - industrial-related expenses,
    - broader environmental/resilience expenses,
    - GDP as an economic outcome.

These were not retained as the preferred specification.

Reasons:

    - Total expenses are too broad and include many spending categories unrelated
      to climate response.

    - Industrial expenses did not match the theoretical mechanism well.

    - The broader environmental/resilience file mixed several categories such as
      infrastructure, sanitation, civil defense, social assistance, basic
      assistance, and urban resilience. This was useful to test, but it made the
      dependent variable less conceptually clean.

    - GDP was dropped because the Climate Risk Index itself contains economic
      structure components. A GDP model could confuse economic size/growth with
      climate risk, especially because industrial exposure can rise together
      with GDP.


================================================================================
10. MAIN CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The environmental expense model is useful, but it does not answer the original
economic-impact question in the way initially intended.

The strongest finding is not:

    "Climate risk increases environmental spending."

The stronger finding is:

    "Environmental spending appears to be more strongly related to transfer
    revenue and fiscal capacity than to short-run changes in climate risk."
    ~a political insight~

And:

    "Higher-risk municipalities may have lower environmental spending capacity,
    suggesting a mismatch between climate risk and fiscal response."

This is still policy-relevant. It suggests that the municipalities with the
highest climate risk may not necessarily be the ones with the fiscal resources
needed to respond.
