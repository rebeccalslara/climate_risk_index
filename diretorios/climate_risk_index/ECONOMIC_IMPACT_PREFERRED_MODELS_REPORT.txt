ECONOMIC IMPACT - PREFERRED MODELS REPORT
Climate Risk Index - Santa Catarina Municipalities
Created: 2026-05-20
Internal use report

================================================================================
1. PURPOSE OF THIS REPORT
================================================================================

This report documents the two preferred economic impact models currently used in
the dashboard:

    1. GDP growth model
    2. Agricultural value-added growth model

The objective is to translate changes in the Climate Risk Index into an
interpretable economic association, while being transparent about the limits of
the model.

This is not a causal proof report. The models estimate associations using panel
data with municipality fixed effects and year fixed effects. The results should
be interpreted as evidence that changes in climate-industrial risk are associated
with subsequent changes in municipal economic performance.


================================================================================
2. WHY THE MODEL CHANGED OVER TIME
================================================================================

The initial idea was to estimate the effect of the full Climate Risk Index on
GDP. This was not adopted as the preferred model because the original index
contains an inverted GDP per capita vulnerability variable:

    pib_pc_inv

Using GDP as the dependent variable while GDP per capita is also embedded in the
index creates a conceptual circularity problem. In that case, the index partially
contains information about the outcome variable.

To reduce this problem, the preferred GDP and agro models use:

    risk_norm_without_GDP

This version of the risk index removes the GDP per capita inverted component from
the vulnerability index before recomputing the aggregate climate risk index.

This does not make the index purely climatic. It still includes industrial
exposure, agriculture sensitivity, energy intensity, and hazard. Therefore it is
best understood as an economic-climate risk index. However, removing GDP makes
the GDP model more defensible.


================================================================================
3. DATA AND SAMPLE
================================================================================

Period used:

    2003-2021

Reason:

    The model uses growth rates and lagged risk, so 2002 is lost because it has
    no previous year growth/lag information. The value-added data used for
    sectoral controls covers up to 2021.

Municipalities:

    295 Santa Catarina municipalities

Observations:

    5,583 municipality-year observations

Key data sources:

    GDP:
        data/raw_data/RAIS/PIB - 2002 a 2023.xlsx
        Converted to real GDP using GDP implicit deflator.

    Industrial value added:
        data/raw_data/IPEADATA/VA Industrial - preços 2010.xls
        Already expressed in 2010 prices. Not deflated again.

    Agricultural value added:
        data/raw_data/IPEADATA/VA Agro.xls
        Already expressed in 2010 prices. Not deflated again.

    Climate Risk Index:
        output/climate_risk_index_2002_2023.csv
        Recomputed internally without the GDP vulnerability component.

Important data validation:

    The VA Industrial and VA Agro files contained one extra municipality name:

        SAO MIGUEL

    This is not part of the 295-municipality Santa Catarina project universe and
    was dropped. SAO MIGUEL DO OESTE was preserved.


================================================================================
4. MODEL 1 - GDP GROWTH MODEL
================================================================================

4.1 Research question
---------------------

Does an increase in the climate-industrial risk index, excluding GDP from the
index, predict lower municipal GDP growth in the following year?


4.2 Equation
------------

    Delta log(real GDP_it) =
        beta * risk_norm_without_GDP_i,t-1
      + gamma * Delta log(VA industrial_it)
      + municipality fixed effects
      + year fixed effects
      + error_it


4.3 Dependent variable
----------------------

    gdp_growth_log

Defined as:

    log(real GDP_t) - log(real GDP_t-1)

This is approximately the real GDP growth rate.


4.4 Main risk variable
----------------------

    risk_norm_without_GDP_lag1

This is the Climate Risk Index without the GDP vulnerability component, lagged by
one year.

Reason for lag:

    Economic effects of climate risk are not expected to occur only
    contemporaneously. A one-year lag is economically plausible because climate
    stress can affect production, investment decisions, supply chains, local
    budgets, and firm behavior with delay.


4.5 Control variable
--------------------

    va_industrial_growth_log

Defined as:

    log(VA industrial_t) - log(VA industrial_t-1)

Reason for inclusion:

    The Climate Risk Index includes industrial exposure. Municipalities with
    growing industrial structure may also have growing GDP. Without controlling
    for industrial value-added growth, the risk coefficient could capture the
    positive relationship between industrial expansion and GDP instead of the
    negative economic pressure associated with climate risk.

Why this control is defensible:

    - GDP contains industrial production.
    - The index includes industrial exposure.
    - Industrial growth is a direct economic channel that must be separated from
      the climate-risk signal.
    - The correlation checks did not indicate that this control fully duplicates
      the risk variable.

Important limitation:

    This control can also be interpreted as a strong control because industrial
    VA is part of municipal GDP. It helps isolate the risk component, but it may
    also absorb part of the economic mechanism if climate risk affects industry
    directly. Therefore, the GDP model should be interpreted as:

        climate-risk association with GDP growth, holding industrial VA growth
        constant.


4.6 Fixed effects
-----------------

The preferred specification uses:

    municipality fixed effects = yes
    year fixed effects         = yes

Municipality fixed effects absorb time-invariant differences between
municipalities, such as geography, long-run economic structure, institutional
capacity, historical productivity, location, and stable vulnerability.

Year fixed effects absorb shocks common to all municipalities in the same year,
such as macroeconomic cycles, national policy changes, inflationary environment,
and broad state/federal conditions.


4.7 Main result
---------------

Preferred specification:

    Municipality FE + year FE

Result for risk_norm_without_GDP_lag1:

    coefficient:        -0.0560
    clustered std err:   0.0266
    p-value:             0.0353
    95% CI:             [-0.1082, -0.0039]

Interpretation for a 0.1 increase in risk:

    -0.559 percentage points in GDP growth

This means that, holding industrial VA growth constant and controlling for
municipality and year fixed effects, an increase of 0.1 in the risk index
without GDP is associated with approximately 0.56 percentage points lower real
GDP growth.

Result for va_industrial_growth_log:

    coefficient:         0.3233
    p-value:             0.0000

Interpretation:

    Industrial VA growth is strongly and positively associated with GDP growth,
    as expected.


4.8 Robustness across fixed effects
-----------------------------------

Risk coefficient across specifications:

    Municipality FE + year FE:
        coefficient = -0.0560
        p-value     = 0.0353
        effect      = -0.559 pp for +0.1 risk

    Municipality FE only:
        coefficient = -0.0812
        p-value     = 0.0006
        effect      = -0.809 pp for +0.1 risk

    Year FE only:
        coefficient = -0.0091
        p-value     = 0.2107
        effect      = -0.091 pp for +0.1 risk

    No fixed effects:
        coefficient = -0.0142
        p-value     = 0.0597
        effect      = -0.142 pp for +0.1 risk

Interpretation:

    The negative sign survives in all specifications. The preferred full fixed
    effects model is statistically significant at the 5% level. This gives the
    GDP model more credibility than the earlier fiscal model.


================================================================================
5. MODEL 2 - AGRICULTURAL VALUE-ADDED GROWTH MODEL
================================================================================

5.1 Research question
---------------------

Is the negative economic association between climate-industrial risk and GDP
partly transmitted through agricultural production?


5.2 Equation
------------

    Delta log(VA agro_it) =
        beta * risk_norm_without_GDP_i,t-1
      + municipality fixed effects
      + year fixed effects
      + error_it


5.3 Dependent variable
----------------------

    va_agro_growth_log

Defined as:

    log(VA agro_t) - log(VA agro_t-1)

This approximates agricultural value-added growth.


5.4 Main risk variable
----------------------

    risk_norm_without_GDP_lag1

The same lagged risk index used in the preferred GDP model.


5.5 Controls
------------

No additional control variables were included in the preferred agro mechanism
model.

Reason:

    This model is used as a mechanism check. The goal is to observe whether the
    climate-industrial risk index is associated with subsequent agricultural VA
    growth. Adding sectoral controls could over-control the channel we are trying
    to measure.


5.6 Fixed effects
-----------------

The model uses:

    municipality fixed effects = yes
    year fixed effects         = yes

This means the model compares changes within the same municipality over time,
net of statewide/common annual shocks.


5.7 Main result
---------------

Result for risk_norm_without_GDP_lag1:

    coefficient:        -0.3745
    clustered std err:   0.0797
    p-value:             0.000003
    95% CI:             [-0.5307, -0.2183]

Interpretation for a 0.1 increase in risk:

    -3.675 percentage points in agricultural VA growth

This is the strongest preferred result. It suggests that the agricultural
channel is highly sensitive to changes in climate-industrial risk.


================================================================================
6. INDUSTRIAL SPILLOVER CHECK
================================================================================

An exploratory spillover model was also tested:

    Delta log(VA industrial_it) =
        beta * Delta log(VA agro_it)
      + municipality fixed effects
      + year fixed effects
      + error_it

Result:

    coefficient on va_agro_growth_log:  0.1156
    p-value:                            0.0003

Interpretation for a 0.1 increase in agro growth:

    +1.163 percentage points in industrial VA growth

This does not prove that climate risk directly affects industry through
agriculture. However, it supports the idea that agricultural performance and
industrial performance are linked in the municipal economy.

Dashboard interpretation:

    The dashboard presents the industrial channel as a spillover, not as a direct
    climate-risk effect on industry.


================================================================================
7. HAZARD-ONLY ROBUSTNESS MODEL
================================================================================

A hazard-only model was also tested:

    Delta log(real GDP_it) =
        beta * hazard_index_it
      + gamma * Delta log(VA industrial_it)
      + municipality fixed effects
      + year fixed effects
      + error_it

Result for hazard_index:

    coefficient:        -0.0562
    p-value:             0.0006
    effect of +0.1:      -0.560 percentage points in GDP growth

Interpretation:

    Current hazard alone is negatively associated with GDP growth, even after
    controlling for industrial VA growth. This supports the idea that the model
    is not only capturing economic structure.

Why it is not the main model:

    The project objective is to communicate the integrated Climate Risk Index.
    Hazard-only is useful as robustness evidence, but the dashboard's main story
    is about the economic meaning of the combined index.


================================================================================
8. WHY THE TWO PREFERRED MODELS ARE DEFENSIBLE
================================================================================

The preferred models are defensible because:

    1. GDP was removed from the risk index before using GDP as the dependent
       variable.

    2. The risk variable is lagged by one year, which is consistent with delayed
       economic effects.

    3. The GDP model controls for industrial VA growth, reducing the risk that
       the coefficient simply reflects industrial expansion.

    4. Both preferred models use municipality fixed effects and year fixed
       effects.

    5. Standard errors are clustered by municipality.

    6. The GDP result is negative and statistically significant in the preferred
       full fixed effects specification.

    7. The agricultural channel result is stronger and highly statistically
       significant.

    8. The hazard-only robustness model also shows a negative association with
       GDP growth.


================================================================================
9. LIMITATIONS AND POSSIBLE ERRORS
================================================================================

9.1 Association, not causality
------------------------------

The models do not prove that climate risk caused GDP or agricultural value added
to fall. They show statistically estimated associations in panel data.

Stronger causal evidence would require an identification strategy such as:

    - exogenous climate shocks,
    - disaster/event records,
    - difference-in-differences design,
    - instrumental variables,
    - or high-frequency event-based data.


9.2 Risk index still contains economic structure
------------------------------------------------

Even without GDP, the risk index still includes industrial exposure and other
economic variables. This is intentional for the project, because the index is an
economic-climate risk index. But it means the coefficient should not be
interpreted as a pure physical climate shock.


9.3 Industrial VA control may be a strong control
-------------------------------------------------

Industrial VA growth helps separate industrial expansion from climate risk, but
it is also part of GDP. If climate risk affects industry directly, this control
may absorb part of the mechanism.

Therefore, the GDP model should be interpreted as:

    risk effect on GDP growth net of industrial VA growth,

not:

    total effect of climate risk on the whole municipal economy.


9.4 Measurement and deflator issues
-----------------------------------

GDP values and VA values come from different sources and concepts:

    GDP:
        Produto Interno Bruto a preços correntes (Mil Reais), later deflated.

    VA industrial/agro:
        R$ (mil), a preços do ano 2010.

The model uses growth rates, which reduces some comparability problems, but does
not eliminate all measurement differences.


9.5 Missing data and municipality creation
------------------------------------------

Balneario Rincao and Pescaria Brava were created later. Pre-creation years should
not be interpreted as missing economic activity. They are structural absence from
the municipal panel.


9.6 Monetary conversion is illustrative
---------------------------------------

The dashboard converts estimated percentage effects into reais using the
municipality's GDP or sectoral VA base. These monetary values are useful for
communication, but they are model-associated estimates, not observed losses.

The value in reais is not directly comparable across municipalities because
larger municipalities naturally have larger economic bases.


================================================================================
10. WHY EARLIER MODELS WERE NOT ADOPTED
================================================================================

10.1 Full Climate Risk Index on GDP
-----------------------------------

This was rejected because the full index includes GDP per capita inverted inside
vulnerability. Using full risk_norm to explain GDP creates a circularity problem.


10.2 GDP level instead of GDP growth
------------------------------------

GDP levels are strongly related to municipality size and structure. Growth rates
are more appropriate for studying oscillations over time.


10.3 Environmental expense model
--------------------------------

The environmental expense model was useful as an internal diagnostic but was not
adopted for dashboard communication.

Preferred fiscal specification:

    log(real_environmental_expense) =
        beta * risk_mean_3yr
      + gamma * log(real_transfer_revenue)
      + municipality FE
      + year FE

Main result:

    risk_mean_3yr coefficient = 0.1563
    p-value                   = 0.9176

Interpretation:

    The risk coefficient was not statistically strong in the full fixed effects
    model.

However, transfer revenue was strong:

    log_real_transfer_revenue coefficient = 0.8590
    p-value                           = 0.0159

This suggests that environmental spending is more directly associated with
fiscal capacity/transfers than with the climate risk index itself.


10.4 Total revenue control
--------------------------

Total revenue was tested but removed from the fiscal model because it did not
improve the risk coefficient and conceptually overlapped with transfer revenue.


10.5 Government expense and revenue growth controls
---------------------------------------------------

Government expenditure/revenue growth controls were tested in GDP growth
explorations. They did not materially improve the preferred model and risked
adding noise or over-controlling fiscal channels not central to the project
story.


10.6 Export growth control
--------------------------

Export growth was tested as a possible control. It was not retained because it
was not statistically strong and did not change the core interpretation of the
preferred model.


10.7 Agro and services VA controls in the GDP model
---------------------------------------------------

Adding agriculture and services VA growth as controls was considered, but it
risks over-controlling the outcome itself. GDP is composed of sectoral value
added. If all main sectors are controlled, the model can become mechanically
close to explaining GDP with its own components.

The chosen compromise was to control industrial VA growth only because the
Climate Risk Index is explicitly industrial and includes industrial exposure.


================================================================================
11. CURRENT DASHBOARD INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

The dashboard should communicate four ideas:

    1. A rise in the climate-industrial risk index is associated with lower GDP
       growth in the following year.

    2. The agricultural channel is stronger: higher risk is associated with
       materially lower agricultural VA growth.

    3. Industrial impact is presented as an indirect spillover, not a direct
       causal estimate.

    4. Monetary values are communication estimates derived from model-associated
       percentage effects and local economic bases.

Suggested language:

    "The estimated monetary values represent the portion of economic variation
    associated with changes in climate risk according to the panel model. They
    should not be interpreted as observed losses or causal damages."


================================================================================
12. FINAL POSITION
================================================================================

The current preferred model set is coherent and defensible for dashboard
communication:

    Model 1:
        GDP growth on lagged risk_norm_without_GDP, controlling for industrial
        VA growth, with municipality and year fixed effects.

    Model 2:
        Agricultural VA growth on lagged risk_norm_without_GDP, with
        municipality and year fixed effects.

Main interpretation:

    The Climate Risk Index is economically meaningful. When the index rises, the
    following year's municipal GDP growth tends to be lower, and the agricultural
    channel appears to be one of the strongest transmission mechanisms.

Main caution:

    The estimates are associations from fixed effects panel models, not causal
    proof of climate damages.
